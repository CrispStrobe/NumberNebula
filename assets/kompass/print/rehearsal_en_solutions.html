1. A bundle count is missing2 points

A collection represents 2734: 2 thousands, an unknown number of hundreds, 13 tens and 4 ones.

a) How many hundreds are missing? (1 pt)

Marking: 6

b) Use a calculation to show that your representation equals 2734. (1 pt)

Marking: 1 point: 2000 + 600 + 130 + 4 = 2734, or an equivalent decomposition.

  • 1 point: 2000 + 600 + 130 + 4 = 2734, or an equivalent decomposition.

Worked solution: 2734 − 2000 − 130 − 4 = 600. The missing count is 6 hundreds.

Marking: 1215

4. Use groups of four2 points

Four packs of 25 tokens contain 100 tokens altogether. There are 36 such packs.

a) How many tokens do 36 packs contain? (1 pt)

Marking: 900

b) Explain a method using groups of four packs. (1 pt)

Marking: 1 point: 36 ÷ 4 = 9 groups of four, and 9 × 100 = 900. Accept an equivalent correct explanation.

  • 1 point: 36 ÷ 4 = 9 groups of four, and 9 × 100 = 900. Accept an equivalent correct explanation.

Worked solution: 36 ÷ 4 = 9; 9 × 100 = 900 tokens.

6. Continue a calculation pattern2 points

A set follows this rule: the first addend increases by 1 each time and the second decreases by 1. It begins 201 + 89, 202 + 88, 203 + 87.

a) Which second addend pairs with 205? (1 pt)

Marking: 85

b) What happens to the sums? Explain. (1 pt)

Marking: 1 point: the sum stays 290 because each increase of 1 is balanced by a decrease of 1.

  • 1 point: the sum stays 290 because each increase of 1 is balanced by a decrease of 1.

Worked solution: 205 + 85 = 290. All sums stay equal because +1 and −1 cancel.

a) What is the largest allowed sum? (1 pt)

Marking: 33

Three boxes contain books. The first has 12 and the second has 15. All we know about the third is that it is blue. Find the total number of books.

a) Can the exact total be determined from this information? (1 pt)

Marking: No, a quantity is missing.

  • 1 point: the number of books in the third/blue box. Its colour or size is insufficient.

9. Join two identical rectangles2 points

Two paper rectangles each measure 2 cm by 3 cm. Join them without overlap along one whole side of equal length. The outer shape must be a rectangle again.

a) Which outer dimensions are possible? Select all, without counting rotations twice. (1 pt)

Marking: 2 cm × 6 cm; 4 cm × 3 cm

b) Describe either valid arrangement. (1 pt)

Marking: 1 point: joining along the 2 cm sides gives 2 × 6 cm; joining along the 3 cm sides gives 4 × 3 cm. Either correct description is enough.

  • 1 point: joining along the 2 cm sides gives 2 × 6 cm; joining along the 3 cm sides gives 4 × 3 cm. Either correct description is enough.

Worked solution: 2 cm × 6 cm and 4 cm × 3 cm are possible. 4 cm × 6 cm would have twice the area of both pieces together.

10. Combine half squares2 points

An area consists of 4 whole unit squares and 2 triangles. Each triangle is exactly half of another unit square. The pieces join without gaps or overlaps.

a) How many whole units of area is that altogether? (1 pt)

Marking: 5

b) Explain how you counted the two triangles. (1 pt)

Marking: 1 point: two halves of the same unit size make one whole unit of area, so 4 + 1 = 5.

  • 1 point: two halves of the same unit size make one whole unit of area, so 4 + 1 = 5.

Worked solution: 4 + ½ + ½ = 5 units of area.

Marking: 0

b) What does this example show about Jona's claim? (1 pt)

  • 1 point: the claim is false. All three sides differ, so no fold creates matching halves. One valid counterexample disproves 'every'.

Worked solution: This scalene triangle has no symmetry axis and is a counterexample to 'every triangle'.

  • 1 point: each column of the view shows only its maximum height; shorter towers behind or in front can have different arrangements.

13. Which nets form a cube?2 points

a) Which shapes are valid cube nets? Select all. (1 pt)

Marking: A; D

b) Why is having six squares alone insufficient? (1 pt)

  • 1 point: the arrangement must create six distinct cube faces; unsuitable layouts overlap or leave the cube open. A correct example using B or C is sufficient.

20. Equally good winning rules2 points

Number cards 1 to 8 are shuffled face down. Every card is equally likely. Alex earns a point for a number greater than 6. Choose a rule giving Bo the same chance of earning a point.

a) Which rule gives Bo the same chance? (1 pt)

Marking: The number is at most 2.

b) Explain using the matching cards. (1 pt)

Marking: 1 point: Alex wins with 7 and 8, Bo with 1 and 2. Both rules have exactly two favourable cards out of eight equally likely cards.

  • 1 point: Alex wins with 7 and 8, Bo with 1 and 2. Both rules have exactly two favourable cards out of eight equally likely cards.

Worked solution: At most 2 gives the same chance: 1 and 2 instead of 7 and 8, so two of eight outcomes each. Even or odd would each have four of eight.
